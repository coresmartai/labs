# Where to start

Read `W04-P08` first. It is the spec. This folder is the benchmark half of
the project; the pipeline half is KnowledgeVault, which you copy in from
`week-04/knowledgevault` and keep working on.

Your project folder is KnowledgeVault plus this folder's contents on top:

    project-08-paperfinder/
    |-- app/                  KnowledgeVault, unchanged to begin with
    |-- tests/                the ten smoke tests; keep them green
    |-- bench/                this folder: papers.json, the two query files, eval.py
    |-- data/sample/          the five PDFs go here (not committed)
    |-- KNOWLEDGEVAULT.md     the pipeline README, renamed out of the way
    |-- README.md             yours, from README.template.md
    `-- NOTES.md              this file

Order of work, roughly:

1. Get the five PDFs. `bench/papers.json` has the URL and the exact filename
   for each. The filename stem becomes the `document_id`, and every golden
   query names one of those five ids, so a paper saved under a different
   name scores zero for every query that targets it.
2. Ingest through the running server, not the CLI, because the embedded
   Qdrant store allows one process at a time:

       uvicorn app.main:app --reload
       curl -X POST "http://localhost:8000/ingest/all?reset=true" > bench/ingest_report.json

   Five papers is 10 to 20 minutes. Do not run `--reset` again afterwards
   unless you mean to re-describe every figure and pay for it twice.
3. `curl http://localhost:8000/stats > bench/stats.json`. Look at the figure
   column. Any paper showing zero figure-description chunks has figures the
   parser cannot see (it extracts embedded raster images, and a figure drawn
   as vector graphics is not one). Note which papers those are now; the
   memo needs to say it, and eval.py will mark those figure queries
   `unreachable` rather than `miss`.
4. `python bench/eval.py`. The given ten, k=5. Read the misses. Each one
   is a specific chunk that lost to a specific other chunk, and
   `POST /retrieve` with the same query shows you which.
5. Write `bench/my_queries.jsonl`: ten queries, at least four figure or
   table. Write the question first, then find the answer chunk, then write
   `must_contain` from what is actually in it. Then run the baseline over
   both sets: `python bench/eval.py --set all --label baseline`. This is
   your before number, and `bench/results_baseline.json` is committed.
6. Change one thing. Chunk size, k, widen, the BM25 tokeniser, the RRF
   constant, the figure describer prompt, the parser. One. Re-run with
   `--set all --label <what-you-changed>` and keep both JSON files.
7. `README.md`: fill in the template. The memo is the part that is graded
   on reasoning, and it is short on purpose.

## What is already done for you

- `bench/golden_queries.jsonl`: ten queries, five prose and five figure,
  over the five papers. Do not edit this file. The reviewer runs it as-is.
- `bench/eval.py`: recall@k overall, by modality, by set, per query; MRR;
  p50 and p95 latency; writes `bench/results_<label>.json`. You can read
  it, and you should, because the hit rule is in there and it is the thing
  your own queries are scored by.
- `bench/papers.json`: the corpus definition.

## What is deliberately missing

There is no worked answer to the misses. Two of the given figure queries
are on the paper whose figures the parser is known to see; the other three
target papers whose figures may be vector-drawn, and finding out which is
part of the project. There is no expected recall number. A baseline that
scores badly and a memo that explains exactly why, with evidence, is worth
more than a high number with no explanation.

## Cost

Five papers, roughly 250 to 400 chunks embedded, and somewhere between 5
and 30 figures described at the mini tier: well under a dollar for a full
ingest. Re-ingesting after a chunker change costs the same again, so make
the change deliberately, not in a loop.
